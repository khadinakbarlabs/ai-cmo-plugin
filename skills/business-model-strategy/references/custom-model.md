# Adapt an unfamiliar or hybrid business

1. Identify who receives value, who pays, who decides and how money/value flows. Ask for an example transaction if the label is unclear.
2. Describe geography, offering, purchase cycle, repeat value, stage, delivery capacity, price/cost scope and relevant restrictions. Do not force a SaaS, ecommerce or founder assumption.
3. Map awareness → evaluation → the actual conversion → useful activation/delivery → repeat value/advocacy. Rename/remove stages when inappropriate; procurement or donation may replace checkout.
4. Set one primary goal plus quality, cost and capacity guardrails. If economics or baseline are unavailable, keep them unknown and propose measurement.
5. Select channels by buyer access, intent, trust, decision complexity, constraints and feasible production. Use the marketing-discipline map; propose unsupported channels as hypotheses.
6. Produce the smallest relevant asset and experiment with owners, required evidence, account/tool dependencies and evaluation timing. For a regulated or unfamiliar domain, obtain current authoritative rules before specific claims or execution.

Output fields: model description, industry, stakeholders, transaction/value flow, primary conversion, repeat-value event, cycle, geography, stage, team/capacity, economics known/unknown, constraints, channel rationale, assets, measurement and next decision.

## Sector-specific claim check

Check nonnumeric claims as carefully as price/performance. Volunteer eligibility (“no experience needed”), training/materials, screening requirements, uses of donations, session availability, staff credentials, supplier certification and procurement access all require actual business evidence. Placeholder fields elsewhere do not substantiate them. Neutral copy can ask an interested person to request the confirmed role/service details without promising eligibility or support that was not supplied.
