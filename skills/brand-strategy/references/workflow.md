# Brand and Positioning working reference

## Output contract

Positioning brief, offer/pricing hypotheses, voice examples and claim register.

Record the business/campaign ID, source dates and input scope. Include the useful artifact plus evidence, unavailable fields, decision and next owner. Treat an estimated outcome as a hypothesis until measured.

## Quality checks

- Does the output answer the requested customer/business job?
- Can another person verify the important facts from the retained evidence?
- Are prerequisites and actual tool capabilities distinguished from completed actions?
- Are unknown metrics marked and recommendations tied to a test or evidence?
- Is the next handoff concrete and editable?

## Recovery

For missing tools or accounts, produce the useful draft/import-based result and identify the specific missing capability. For empty data, retain collection scope and explain the absence rather than inventing a finding. For partial results, mark the missing portion and avoid extrapolating to the whole market.

## Offer and pricing decisions

Write a segment × job × alternative matrix. For each proposed offer record the value metric, delivery cost, margin constraints, buying friction, evidence source and validation method. Use a price hypothesis rather than a fabricated willingness-to-pay number. Keep brand facts/claims separately from creative hooks. A competitor's lower price is not sufficient reason to lower the business's price.

## Covered variants

- Ideal customer profile
- Positioning and differentiation
- Offer and pricing strategy
- Brand voice and messaging
- Brand and creative review

## Positioning versus validated fit

A known audience supports “For freelancers managing invoices and expense exports.” It does not establish “Built around how freelancers actually work,” which implies workflow validation. Check qualitative positioning language as well as numeric/feature claims; remove implied studies, customer consensus or proven fit unless evidence supports them.

## Brand-building measurement

Brand/category development may target awareness, consideration, trust or distribution before immediate response. Define suitable research or first-party proxies, source/sample, expected lag and business connection; do not dismiss every non-conversion measure or equate awareness with revenue. Positioning is a hypothesis until customer/market evidence supports fit.
