## Loop measurement

Example calculation: 200 eligible activated users send 300 unique qualified invitations; 30 invited recipients become activated. Invitations/user = 1.5; invite-to-activation = 0.10; cohort K = 0.15. Do not report K = 1.5 from invitations alone. State deduplication, observation window, recipient overlap, external seed audience, repeat-sharing behavior and cycle time. This sample is illustrative, not a benchmark. Diagnose recipient value, delivery, activation and retention before escalating incentives.

## Missing recipient-level evidence

When only aggregate counts are supplied, K is a conditional arithmetic estimate assuming valid cohort membership and distinct attributed activations. A “unique invitations” column alone does not prove activation deduplication or prohibit recipients receiving several invites. Do not claim duplicates were checked without the underlying records. Project future growth/decay only as an explicit scenario with stable-rate and cycle-time assumptions; do not present it as a forecast.

## Bias direction

Invitation records, distinct recipients and distinct activated recipients are different counts. Recipient overlap does not by itself prove an inflated conversion rate: numerator and denominator quality are both unknown. Without recipient-level attribution and deduplication, report a conditional aggregate K and do not assert the direction or size of bias.
